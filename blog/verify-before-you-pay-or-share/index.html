Verify before you pay or share: do your own research

Reach helps you find and contact partners. It does not vouch for them. Here are the checks to make before you send money, assets or sensitive details.

The Alchemist
November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Anyone can list an option in a public library, and a long list of closed deals does not prove that a new seller is real. Reach finds people and keeps your outreach organised. It does not check who they are. That check is yours, and it takes a few minutes.

When it matters most

Verify before you do any of these: agree a price, send money, hand over a login or a document, give someone access to an account or a page, or transfer a domain, site or handle. Before that point you are only talking.

Six checks that catch most problems

1. They are who they say they are. Match the name, website and social handle in at least two independent places. Look up the age of the domain, and make sure the email address matches the website and is not a free address or a look-alike spelling. Search their name with words like "scam" and "review", and read reviews on sites they do not control.

2. Their audience or traffic is real. Ask for current analytics, then compare with what you can see in public. Watch for sudden follower jumps, very low engagement for the size, and generic comments. For a website, check traffic with an independent tool and open the pages they say will carry you.

3. You spoke to them on a channel you found yourself. Use the contact on their official site or verified profile, not only the link in their message. Get on a short video call. Ask for two past clients and contact them yourself.

4. You know how the money moves. Prefer a card, a marketplace checkout or an escrow service. Be very careful with wire transfers, crypto, gift cards and instant payment apps, because they are hard to reverse. Start small with someone new, or pay in milestones. If payment details change, confirm them by phone before you send anything.

5. You share only what you must. Never share passwords, one-time codes, API keys, seed phrases, or ID, bank or card numbers. If a partner needs access, add them with a limited role using the platform's own invite feature instead of giving a login. Sign an NDA before you share anything confidential.

6. It is in writing. Price, deliverables, dates, usage rights and what happens if something is not delivered. An email you both confirm is enough to start.

Red flags

Pressure to decide now. A price far below the market. Payment wanted before anything is shown. A request for your password or a code sent to your phone. A "verification" or "release" fee. A payment larger than the price, with a request to send the difference back. A refusal to get on a call. A website that is almost, but not exactly, a name you know.

What Reach does to help

Library options are marked as not verified. When you move a deal to negotiating or won, or schedule a payment, Reach asks you to go through the checks and records what you ticked on the deal. If a draft asks for passwords or payment details, you get a warning. None of this replaces your own research, and Reach cannot promise that a partner is genuine.

Your call

Verifying partners is your responsibility. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. If a deal is large, ask a professional to look at the contract.
