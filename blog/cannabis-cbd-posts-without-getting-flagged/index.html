Posting about cannabis or CBD without getting flagged

The one rule that works across every platform: act like a publisher or teacher, not a shop.

The Alchemist
November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Every major social platform restricts cannabis. Where they differ is in how much education they tolerate. The safe approach is the same everywhere: teach, report and explain. Do not sell, and do not show use.

What gets accounts in trouble

  • Prices, deals, menus, delivery, "order now", promo codes or any call to buy.
  • Showing or glamorising use, slang for use, and paraphernalia.
  • Anything that could appeal to children, such as candy-like or cartoon treatment of the product.
  • Health claims: saying it treats, cures or relieves anything.
  • Hashtags that platforms restrict or that read as promotion, such as slang for use or "shop" and "sale" tags.

Platform by platform

  • Instagram and Facebook. Educational, news and advocacy content is allowed. Direct promotion or sale is not.
  • TikTok. Education only. No promoting or glorifying use, no paraphernalia, no unpaid product promotion.
  • X. Some licensed advertisers can run ads in certain US states, but organic posts should still not promote or encourage use.
  • YouTube. Educational, documentary and scientific context is allowed, but you may not link to places that sell it, and dispensary promotion is age-restricted. See YouTube's own policy page.
  • LinkedIn. Industry news, research and policy, in a professional voice. No promotion of sale or use.
  • Pinterest. Informational content is allowed. No product sales or therapeutic claims.

These summaries come from each platform's public policies and trade guides, checked on November 1, 2026. Meta also publishes its drugs and pharmaceuticals policy. Policies change, so check the current page before you rely on any of this.

What works instead

  • Explain how something works: how a terpene differs from a cannabinoid, what a lab report shows, what a new law changes.
  • Report news and policy changes, with the source named.
  • Show the place, the people, the craft, the science. Not the product being used.
  • Use educational hashtags such as ones about science, policy or education.
  • Add a plain line: "For adults 21+ where legal. Educational only, not medical advice."

What the Alchemist does for you

When a brand or campaign is about cannabis, CBD or hemp, the writer is told to write education or news only, and the post is checked for selling words, claims, use references and risky hashtags. Not legal advice

Cannabis law differs by country, state and city. This post is general information about platform rules. Check your local rules and each platform's current policy.
