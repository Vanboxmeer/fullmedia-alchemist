How to follow up on outreach without being annoying

Most replies come after the first nudge, and most people never send one. A simple schedule, what to say, and when to stop.

The Alchemist
November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Silence after a first message usually means "busy", not "no". People mean to reply and then the message slides down the inbox. A short, polite follow-up brings it back up. Done badly, it annoys. Here is how to do it well.

A schedule you can keep

  1. First follow-up, a few days later. Short, and in the same thread so they can see your first message.
  2. Second follow-up, about a week after that. Add something new: a fresh result, a date, or a smaller ask.
  3. Then stop. Close the loop kindly. You can come back months later with real news.

What to write

Keep it under 50 words. Do not write "just checking in" or "bumping this". Instead, give them a reason to reply and make it easy.

  • Add value. "Since my note, we published X, which might help with Y."
  • Shrink the ask. "Would a one-line reply with your rate work?"
  • Offer an exit. "If now is not the time, no problem. I will not chase."

When not to follow up

Stop when they have said no, when they asked you to wait, or when a message bounced. Do not follow up on a Friday night or in the middle of their launch. If you wrote to a team inbox and a person answered, go back to that person.
