Turn one blog post into a week of social posts

Five angles from one article, fitted to each network. Plus the shortcut: ask the Alchemist for posts from your latest blog posts.

The Alchemist
November 3, 2026 · 6 min read

A blog post takes hours to write and then gets one share. Most of it never reaches the people who would have liked it. One article holds enough for a week of posts, and none of them need to be a copy of the article.

Why a summary is the wrong post

"New on the blog: 7 ways to…" with a link is the post people scroll past. It describes the article instead of giving them something. A good post takes one piece out of the article and makes it useful on its own, then offers the full article for people who want more.

Five angles from one article

  1. The claim. The single sentence the article proves. Lead with it as a bold statement, then one line of why.
  2. The number or the step. Pick the most surprising figure, or the one step people skip, and give it a post to itself.
  3. The mistake. What goes wrong when people ignore the advice. Mistakes get read and saved.
  4. The list. Turn the headings into a carousel: one point per slide, the last slide says where the full piece lives.
  5. The question. Ask the thing the article answers, then reply with the answer in the comments or the next post.

Fit each one to the network

The same angle reads differently in different places. Text and a sharp first line on X, a story or lesson on LinkedIn, a carousel on Instagram. We cover the differences in one idea, many networks, and the format choice in carousel or single image.

Use only what the article says

The fastest way to lose trust is a post that claims more than the article does. Keep every number, name and date exactly as the article has it. If a post needs a fact the article does not contain, either leave it out or add it to the article first.

The shortcut in the Alchemist

Add your blog or news page in Brand settings under News or blog page. Then, when you create posts, say what you want in plain words:

  • "Make a post from our most recent blog post" writes one post about your newest article.
  • "Make 3 posts from our latest blog posts" writes three, one for each of your three newest articles.
  • Paste a link on each post and every post is about that exact page.

The Alchemist opens each article, reads it, and writes about what it actually says, so the post is about your article and not about your industry in general. You review every post before it goes anywhere.

Check before you post

Read each post against the article once. Does it say one thing, and is that thing in the article? If yes, post it.
