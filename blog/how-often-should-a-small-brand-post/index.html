How often should a small brand post? The honest answer

There is no magic number. Here is a way to pick one you can keep, and the signals that tell you to change it.

The Alchemist
November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

You will find confident numbers for this question on every marketing blog, and they disagree. The honest answer is that the best schedule is the one you can keep for a year, on the networks where your customers actually are.

Start smaller than you think

A brand posting three times a week on two networks for six months beats a brand posting daily on five for three weeks. Pick the networks first. Two is plenty to start, and one is fine. Then pick a number you could still hit in your busiest week.

A sensible starting point

Your timeTry this
About 20 minutes a weekOne batch a week: 2 or 3 posts on your best network
About an hour a week3 to 5 posts across two networks, plus time to reply
A few hours a weekDaily on one network, a few a week elsewhere, and time to review results

These are starting points, not rules. If you are unsure, begin at the top row and move down only when it feels easy.

Frequency is not the same as reach

More posts do not help if each one says nothing. One post with a clear idea, a real fact and a clear next step is worth more than three fillers. If you have to choose between more posts and better posts, choose better.

Signals to change your schedule

  • Raise it when your posts keep getting replies and saves, and you have good ideas waiting.
  • Lower it when quality drops, you repeat yourself, or you are skipping weeks.
  • Move it when one network clearly gets more response than another. Spend the time where it works.

Check what actually works

Save the results on each post for a month, then look at which networks and formats earned their place. The What worked where table does that for you. A simple routine to keep it going is in the 20-minute weekly routine.

The rule that matters

Never promise yourself a schedule you will break. A missed week costs more than a slower pace.
