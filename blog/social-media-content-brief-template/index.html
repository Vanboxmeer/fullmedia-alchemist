A one-page content brief for social posts (copy this template)

Eight short answers that turn "we should post something" into posts that sound like you. Use it with a team, a freelancer or an AI writer.

The Alchemist
November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Most weak social posts have a weak brief behind them. "Post something about the sale" gives a writer nothing to work with, and the result is generic. A brief takes five minutes and fixes most of it.

The template

QuestionWhat to write
1. GoalOne result you want: sign-ups, bookings, replies, awareness of one thing
2. AudienceWho exactly, and what they care about right now
3. The one ideaOne sentence a reader could repeat to a friend
4. ProofThe real facts that back it up: a number, a date, a customer line, a detail only you know
5. The askThe one thing you want them to do, and the link it goes to
6. VoiceThree words for how it should sound, and one thing it should never sound like
7. Must include, must avoidRequired wording, hashtags, and topics or claims to stay away from
8. Where and whenWhich networks, and the dates it must go out by

A filled-in example

A small bakery, launching a pre-order for holiday boxes.

  1. Goal: 40 pre-orders before December 10.
  2. Audience: locals who send gifts to coworkers and family.
  3. One idea: a box you can order in a minute that arrives ready to give.
  4. Proof: six flavours, delivery within the city on Dec 18 to 20, box price on the order page.
  5. Ask: order on the pre-order page.
  6. Voice: warm, plain, a bit playful. Never pushy.
  7. Include or avoid: include the order deadline. Avoid "limited stock" unless it is true.
  8. Where and when: Instagram and Facebook, three posts between Dec 1 and Dec 9.

The two answers people skip

Proof is what separates a post from an advert. If you cannot name a fact, the post will be filler. Must avoid saves you the awkward edit later, so list the claims you cannot make.

How the Alchemist uses it

The brand profile holds the parts that stay the same: who you are, who it is for, how you sound, the facts it may use and what to never do. The campaign holds the goal and the dates. A short request or a link for each post holds the one idea. Together they are the same brief, filled in once and reused. The Alchemist writes from those facts and does not invent others.
