A 20-minute weekly social media routine that actually survives a busy week

One sitting, four jobs, one week of posts. A routine for small teams, with the Alchemist doing the heavy part.

The Alchemist
October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Consistency beats intensity. A small brand that posts every week for a year will beat one that posts daily for a month. The trick is a routine small enough to survive your worst week.

The 20 minutes

  1. Minutes 0 to 5: plan one campaign. Pick one thing worth talking about this week: an offer, a launch, a lesson, a customer story. Write one line about it in the campaign brief. Say it out loud if that is faster.
  2. Minutes 5 to 10: let the Alchemist draft. Transmute the week. It builds posts from your own site and brand facts, so you are editing, not starting from a blank page.
  3. Minutes 10 to 15: review like an editor. Does each post say one thing? Is every fact yours? Fix the headline first, because the headline carries the post.
  4. Minutes 15 to 20: approve, post, reply. Copy each fitted caption to its network, or schedule it. Then answer every comment on last week's posts.

Three rules that keep it going

One idea per week. It is easier to write seven versions of one idea than seven ideas. Same day, same time. Put it in your calendar like a meeting. Look back monthly. Save results on your posts, then read the What worked where table once a month and keep what earns its place.

If you only have 10 minutes

Skip planning, reuse last week's best-performing format, and spend the time replying to comments. Replies are the cheapest reach you have.
