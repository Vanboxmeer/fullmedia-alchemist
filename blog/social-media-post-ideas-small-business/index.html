Nothing to post? 25 post ideas a small business can use this week

Five kinds of post that always have something to say, with five prompts each. Pick one, add a real detail, and you have a post.

The Alchemist
November 3, 2026 · 6 min read

"What should we post?" is the question that stops most small brands. The answer is almost never a new idea. It is a real detail from your week, shaped into one of a few kinds of post that people like to read.

Teach

  1. The question customers ask you most, answered in three lines.
  2. A mistake you see people make, and the fix.
  3. A myth in your field, and what is actually true.
  4. A quick how-to for something you do every day without thinking.
  5. A simple checklist: what to do before you buy, book or start.

Show

  1. Behind the scenes of one ordinary task.
  2. A before and after of something you made or fixed.
  3. The tools or ingredients you use, and why you chose them.
  4. Your workspace, your team, or the person behind the brand.
  5. One day in the life, in four pictures.

Prove

  1. A customer result, told with their permission.
  2. A real review, with the detail that mattered.
  3. A number from your own work: how many, how fast, how long.
  4. A before and after story of a customer problem.
  5. A thing you got wrong, and what you changed.

Ask

  1. A poll on a choice you are really trying to make.
  2. "What would you like us to cover next?"
  3. This or that: two options, pick one.
  4. A fill-in-the-blank about your field.
  5. Ask for a tip, and share the best ones next week.

Announce

  1. Something new: a product, a service, a date.
  2. A change of hours, place or price, stated plainly.
  3. An event you are going to, with the real date.
  4. A deadline for an offer that really ends.
  5. A thank-you to someone who helped you.

Make any of them better

Add one detail only you know: a name, a number, a date, a place. That detail is the difference between a post and filler. Then fit it to the network, as in one idea, many networks, and use the one-page brief if a teammate or tool is writing it.

In the Alchemist, put the real details in your brand knowledge once. Every batch can then draw on them, and it will not invent facts you did not give it.
