Equivalent exchange: credits, models and the six-hour window

How credits work, what the Fast, Pro and Max models cost, and why every plan has an allowance per six hours.

The Alchemist
October 10, 2026 · 4 min read · updated October 30

An alchemist trades something of equal value for what he creates. In Fullmedia Alchemist the medium is credits: you see the price before you press Transmute, and you only pay for what actually arrives.

One credit, one slide

A credit buys one slide written with the Fast model. A single-image post costs 1 credit and a 3-slide carousel costs 3. Every slide carries new information, never a repeat of the one before.

Fast, Pro and Max

Every plan can use all three writing models. Pick one when you create posts and the price updates before you commit.

Each post’s picture adds 1 credit: a new picture of your character, or for brands without one, an AI picture in the art style you chose. If the model you picked isn't available at that moment, the Alchemist writes with Fast and charges Fast.

Redos

Every post gets free redos: 1 on Free, 2 on Starter, 3 on Growth and 5 on Agency. After that, a redo costs credits like a new post.

Monthly credits and the six-hour window

Credits refill every month. Each plan can also use a set amount every six hours, so the service stays fast for everyone. The windows reset four times a day, at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC.

Fair trade

If an image fails to render, or a character picture can’t be made, its credits come straight back. You never pay for something that didn’t arrive.

Rising through the ranks

Every post you make, approve and put out earns XP, and you climb from Initiate to Grand Alchemist. The climb is slow on purpose. A rank should mean you've actually done the work.

Update, October 30

Starter and Growth now include unlimited campaigns, since campaigns are organization, and credits are the real limit. Voice is a fair-use feature and does not use credits. A text, thread or poll version costs 1 credit, and a failed batch is refunded.
